Inside the book

The rhythm of a year.

Every station follows the same shape: a command, a page, and at the Full Moon, a chance to look back.

Station one of forty-eight

One command a week, plainly put.

Each station opens with a single command of Jesus, printed in the King James words, beside one prompt. No commentary, nothing to interpret — just the word, and room enough to answer it in your own hand.

Station 1 · Repent · Matthew 4:17Turning is a physical act. Stand and turn your whole body slowly toward something you have been avoiding, then sit and write what your body did: the breath, the jaw, the stomach. Then write what it would take for your body to turn the rest of the way.

STATION 1 · ONE DEEP PROMPT · A REAL SPREAD

For a daily habit

Or seven lines, one for each day.

Some weeks want a single sitting. Others want a smaller door you can walk through daily. Seven short lines, dated in the margin, carry the same command through the week instead of asking for it all at once.

  1. Where in my body do I feel what I am avoiding?
  2. Turn my shoulders. What changes in my breath?
  3. Where do I brace before I do what is right?
  4. What does my body do when I lie to myself?
  5. Stand. Face the other way. What do I feel?
  6. What did my body know before my mind did?
  7. Dear Body, I am turning toward...

STATION 1 · SEVEN DAILY LINES · A REAL SPREAD

Every twelfth page

A page to look back on.

At the Full Moon the page turns inward: an opening Psalm, a closing letter, and room to reflect on the moon just past — what four weeks of one command actually did.

THE FULL MOON REFLECTION · A REAL SPREAD

Between the stations

Four midpoints ask what changed.

Halfway between each pair of stations, a dated panel returns to the same question: what has changed since. Not a new command — a record, so a year of writing reads back as change, not only a list of answers.
